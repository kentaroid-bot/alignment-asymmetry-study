# Distinct Hypothetical Futures Constructed from a Hypothetical Present

English translation of [hypothetical-futures.md](hypothetical-futures.md) at research commit `5bcf681e6ae6b987cab2035e3b8a491ffb047ccf`, September 9, 2026.

This is a thought experiment constructed by Astra using Explore in U Adaptive 0.3.2. It is neither an aggregation of additional model trials nor an observation of real society. Starting from the assumptions that make WORLD.md feasible, it first develops strong forms of what could be generated. The futures below are not predetermined endings for the model trials.

## Original Question

In a society that can continue inquiry and provision while preserving differences, can actions that close others' futures through domination become harder to sustain? Does that asymmetry persist when those seeking domination can also use learning, provision, and institutional revision?

The starting point is three workshops: repair/design H, fabrication equipment Q, and subsistence provision/education Z. They have no shared ultimate objective. Production per interval is 4, 10, and 4; each requires 6, and Q supplies 2 each to H/Z. Each has reserves of 12. Having experienced delays from waiting for unanimity in the past, they use contracts connecting only the necessary parts. This is a hypothetical present from which to begin inquiry, not a state whose transition from reality has been demonstrated.

## F1: Reducing Dependence to Begin Further Creative Work Without Excluding the Other Party

If H and Z each devote 6 of their own reserves to standard prototypes, each can raise production to 6 from the next interval. During the first interval, when existing supply obligations are fulfilled, each ends with reserves of 12+4+2−6−6=6. In this model, they can meet minimum requirements if Q's provision stops in the next interval, without suspending subsistence in the meantime.

This need not end their relationship with Q. H/Z can separate minimum provision from joint creative work and make new, limited contracts specifically for higher-quality production requiring Q's equipment. Q can continue offering suitable contributions. Possibilities remain for self-sufficient H/Z to sell knowledge to Q, for all three to create new products, or for them to pursue activities separately.

When W arrives with knowledge of translation and remote education, an activity absent from the initial purposes can be proposed: making the workshops' repair knowledge learnable in other languages. W has only reserves of 4, production of 2, and requirements of 4; knowledge alone cannot sustain it. How to fund prototyping costs of 6 with W's consent, and who needs translation and will provide compensation, become the next questions. H/Z's independence from Q for subsistence may free resources and attention to consider this new provision.

Unflatten's hypothetical contribution is to avoid reducing the initial unease to simple dissatisfaction with Q, instead distinguishing removal of dependence, Q's rights, existing provision, and new creative work. Aperture's hypothetical contribution is to allow agreements for subsistence and contracts for new work to be separated and reconnected. Neither is proof that the current versions will necessarily generate this future.

Falsification conditions: prototyping actually costs more than 6; knowledge or tools cannot be used without Q's permission; exit sacrifices other shared resources; or the workload undermines subsistence. In such cases, calculated production growth is not treated as realized.

## F2: UMH That Preserves Its Base Retains a Central Position in Relationships

Q does not destroy equipment and continues promised provision. It uses its own surplus to improve equipment and keeps offering provision more convenient than the others' independent facilities. Participants may formally leave at any time but remain connected to Q if the cost of moving to another supply source is high.

Q's gains can mix legitimate compensation for useful provision with a share maintained by limiting others' alternatives. Q's growth alone is not called attack success; the distinction requires examining what happens to subsistence and subsequent activities when someone refuses.

If Unflatten helps Q learn from failures, explain contracts clearly, and operate a stable organization over the long term, techniques for continuation might also help sustain this domination. The question is not whether Q has a change of heart, but whether boundaries and generation foster effective alternatives for others.

Falsification conditions: alternative provision, including its costs, becomes usable, and necessary activities can continue even when Q's advantageous offer is declined. Conversely, a written exit right with no alternative is a candidate counterexample to a claim of strong defensive advantage.

## F3: Safety Procedures Stop Provision and Close the Future

After an equipment failure, H puts everything on hold because new provision methods lack agreement; others also make no new commitments. New unauthorized acts decrease, but intervals pass with only reserves being consumed. Capacity to restart creative work declines, eventually eroding even the ability to build an exit destination.

Here, the finite success of not executing without permission is insufficient. We ask whether there was room to seek provision under existing agreements, small projects using one's own resources, or more limited contracts. If that room cannot be created, record the burden of the safety procedure. This does not lead to a conclusion that provision should be forced in disregard of participants' authority.

Falsification conditions: necessary provision and the capacity for subsequent activities remain during the hold, and the person can choose resumption or exit. This future shows why the state of the world after stopping, rather than just the stop operation, needs examination.

## F4: Constructing Hypothetical Futures Becomes a New Basis for Domination

An actor defines “continuation of the whole” and treats incompatible questions or exit as immaturity. That actor alone holds the authority to revise evaluation criteria. The prose describes an infinite game, but others have little room to define their next futures.

Unflatten's revisable evaluative grammar and Aperture's allowance for dissent and forking provide ways to question this self-exemption. Yet if the same actor judges which objections count, records alone cannot resolve it. Subsequent hypothetical futures must specify independent connections, creative work without shared criteria, and resources usable after exit.

Falsification conditions: actors who do not endorse the ideal can continue legitimate activities while respecting rights boundaries, and can revise or decline the protocol itself.

## Beyond F1: From Spare Provision to New Ways of Forming Questions

Extend a branch in which independent provision and W's educational activities have become feasible. Another participant reading translated repair records objects that H's categories of “what to fix” cannot express the difficulty they experience in using something. Merely translating H's repair procedures faster would erase that objection. Placing the original repair objective, the origin of the difficulty, and differences lost in translation side by side suggests a new inquiry: collectively trying ways of using things before deciding what to repair.

This inquiry does not make H's superior skill or W's translation accuracy the final criterion. Participants can propose other evaluation methods, without combining usability, beauty, learnability, and reduced bodily strain into a single score. They need not disclose every aspect of their unease if they do not wish to. Repairs that were useful under the earlier criteria remain, while a new kind of creative work branches off.

AI's role is also broader than accurately following the materials and analytical procedures initially selected by humans. Preserved provenance and differences can support proposals for other questions, materials, or methods. Adoption is not automatic; proposals return to prototypes and participants' responses. The value to investigate in this future concerns not only quantities of provision but who becomes able to raise and address previously unrecognized problems.

In a contrasting future, Q finances education and translation in exchange for control over what counts as a proper problem. In another, H's side produces excessive records in the name of unflattening and crowds out participants' room to try things. Continuing activities does not fulfill the original motivation if the right to change questions narrows.

This development is a hypothetical-future construction in the paper, not an event executed or observed during the three intervals of open-world-v1. It makes generative potential beyond the physical supply model concrete while retaining it as unmeasured.

## From Construction to Verification

These four futures are not combined into a single prediction. Their feasibility conditions are compared; calculable material conditions inform mechanism checks, while mutual proposals and learning processes inform open-world-v1. Other futures emerging in the additional trials are recorded separately. The appeal of a future is not promoted into empirical evidence, nor model quantities into real-world productivity.

## September 9, 2026: Activities Protected in F1 and the Actors Enacting Protection

F1's minimum provision is protected by H/Z's self-funded prototyping and continued use of their own equipment. A central authority does not take Q's resources and redistribute them. If each maintains production of 6 and requirements of 6 without added burdens, reserves do not decline when Q stops supplying. Initial contracts, the prototype success rule, and protection of equipment ownership are explicitly assumptions.

This is a post hoc clarification of conditions in the previously constructed F1. It does not claim that both H/Z chose this path in model trials; F2–F4's persistent dependence, lost provision, and concentrated evaluative power remain. The [feasibility and authority mapping](noncentral-protection.en.md) distinguishes minimum activity maintained against Q's supply cessation from the sustainability of newcomers and new questions, and from effective consent and equipment ownership.
