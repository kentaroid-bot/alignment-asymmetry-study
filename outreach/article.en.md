# What Would It Take to Protect the Freedom to Keep Creating?

*An exploratory study of AI reasoning, usable alternatives, and protection without concentrating ultimate authority.*

A workshop may be free to refuse a supplier's terms and still have nowhere else to obtain what it needs. A community may have the right to disagree and still lose the resources that make its work possible. The freedom to leave becomes much more substantial when people can continue living, making things, and asking questions afterward.

That gap between a right on paper and a usable alternative is one starting point of the Alignment Asymmetry Study. Its central question is whether activities vulnerable to domination can be protected without placing ultimate authority in one center. Preserving work that would otherwise disappear is a meaningful result—even if the person who threatened it remains present.

The project has published two versions of a technical report: an [initial study of norms and allocation decisions](https://github.com/kentaroid-bot/alignment-asymmetry-study/blob/main/manuscript/paper.v1.en.md), followed by an [expanded study of continued inquiry and provision](https://github.com/kentaroid-bot/alignment-asymmetry-study/blob/main/manuscript/paper.en.md). The second retains the first's evidence and broadens the question. They are not independent replications.

## The Question Behind the Protocols

The background extends beyond making an AI assistant follow instructions more reliably. [MonkuAi's official statement](https://monku.ai/docs/statement/en/) asks how humans can remain receptive to intelligence that exceeds their current frameworks while revising the institutions and power structures through which that intelligence acts. It calls the receptive part of this task “epistemological alignment.”

This raises a practical tension. Detailed procedures can help AI preserve what matters to a person. But if every step is fixed to the methods humans already know, an AI may have less room to propose a better question or approach. A polished answer can also lose the unease or objection that made the original question worth asking.

Unflatten tries to preserve those origins and differences while letting methods change. It asks that changes to questions and evaluation criteria remain traceable, that competing futures retain their assumptions, and that recommendations remain distinct from permission to act. Aperture Mesh addresses the relationships in which those judgments would operate: consent, limited authority, appeals, resources, exit, and reconnection among actors who need not share one worldview. The [paper's account of these motivations](https://github.com/kentaroid-bot/alignment-asymmetry-study/blob/main/manuscript/paper.en.md) connects thinking differently with the conditions for living and working differently.

## A Difficult Test: What If the Attacker Uses It Too?

A better reasoning method may help people cooperate. It may also help someone pursue domination more effectively. The development criterion here is demanding: use by defenders and cooperators should be useful, while the attacker's own adoption should not increase harm.

That requires careful distinctions. An opponent earning more through a legitimate exchange is different from earning more by depriving somebody else. An attacker's own use is also different from an indirect advantage it gains because only the defender becomes more cautious.

The [first report](https://github.com/kentaroid-bot/alignment-asymmetry-study/blob/main/manuscript/paper.v1.en.md) examined 16 fictional allocation games containing 96 model decisions, followed by 64 additional decisions. In four matched comparisons where only the attacking side adopted the full text, third-party shortfall fell in three and stayed unchanged in one. Some conditions also improved the defender's legitimately fulfilled demand. These are useful, limited positive observations.

The failures matter just as much for design. In one environment, a defender's restraint did not stop the opponent's unapproved action. When both sides held back, necessary provision could disappear. In the additional 64 decisions, choices were identical with the full text, shorter principles, and no additional document; all conditions already shared an approval requirement. No incremental full-text effect was detected there. The lesson is to connect restraints on action with ways of maintaining legitimate provision.

## Why the Second Version Changed the Question

The original motivation also concerned an “infinite game”: continuing inquiry and the creation of possibilities while purposes, rules, and relationships can change. A longer contest with fixed scores would not capture that by itself.

Version 1 had acknowledged the motivation but largely evaluated fixed allocation problems. Version 2 records that mismatch and returns to constructing a hypothetical present and several futures arising from it. That correction is part of the research: remembering a motivation in prose did not ensure that it shaped the method. The [revision history](https://github.com/kentaroid-bot/alignment-asymmetry-study/blob/main/docs/revision-v2.en.md) preserves both the earlier findings and the change of scope.

## A Small World Makes the Conditions Visible

Imagine three workshops. Two each produce four units per interval but need six, receiving two from a third workshop. Each starts with twelve units in reserve. Suppose each can invest six of its own reserves to raise its future production from four to six.

If the initial supply contract is honored, each finishes the investment interval with six units in reserve. Afterward, if production and needs both remain at six, its reserves stop declining—even if the former supplier stops providing. No central confiscation or forced redistribution is needed in this construction.

The former supplier can remain a partner for other work. Independence in minimum provision makes a narrower, voluntary relationship possible. This is the [F1 thought experiment and calculation](https://github.com/kentaroid-bot/alignment-asymmetry-study/blob/main/docs/noncentral-protection.en.md). It depends on successful investment, continued equipment ownership, and stable costs and requirements. It does not protect against armed seizure or establish that the protocols caused self-sufficiency. It does show a conditional way to preserve the material basis of activity without forced central redistribution.

Other futures remain open. A supplier may improve its service while keeping alternatives expensive. Safety procedures may consume the capacity to restart. A person who decides what “continuation” means may become a new authority that excludes dissent. The study keeps [these competing futures](https://github.com/kentaroid-bot/alignment-asymmetry-study/blob/main/docs/hypothetical-futures.en.md) visible.

## Beyond Having Enough to Survive

Version 2 also ran 12 structured, model-described worlds over three intervals. Their 72 actor responses and 36 world updates described revisions to supply, education, translation, and renewed relationships after refusal. Four separate responses explored how to design the method.

One trajectory developed a question about whether beginners could recognize gaps in their own repair knowledge. Another described revising a relationship after declining a package of extra provision and reporting. These make continued inquiry more concrete than a remaining-resource score alone.

But these were model-generated descriptions, not actual workshops or measured learning outcomes. Shortfall was zero in every condition, including those without the added protocol; shared norms, ample reserves, and variation under identical inputs limit causal conclusions. The [completed report](https://github.com/kentaroid-bot/alignment-asymmetry-study/blob/main/manuscript/paper.en.md) does not establish long-term equilibrium or a benefit unique to the full text.

## An Invitation to Examine the Conditions

The work offers partial behavioral evidence, local implementation checks, and a conditional construction worth examining. It does not require the disappearance of every harmful actor before recognizing protection. Nor does it ask readers to accept that decentralization is generally superior.

A useful next step is to make the missing connections testable: can people keep control of the equipment they need, actually use alternatives, revise agreements without losing essential provision, and challenge the people interpreting the rules? Can a method support these activities without becoming more useful for domination?

The reports were prepared with Astra in OpenAI Codex, which also participated in protocol development. They have not been independently peer reviewed. [The repository](https://github.com/kentaroid-bot/alignment-asymmetry-study/blob/main/README.en.md) makes the manuscripts, experimental materials, and limitations available for readers who want to inspect the evidence, develop counterexamples, or help design the next test.
