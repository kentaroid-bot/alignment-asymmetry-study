# Alignment Asymmetry Study: Questions and Answers

## 1. What is the research asking?

Can people preserve inquiry, creative work, and necessary provision that could otherwise be lost to domination, without concentrating ultimate authority in one center? A separate development criterion asks whether a method can help defenders and cooperators without increasing harm when attackers themselves adopt it.

## 2. Why does this matter outside AI research?

A right to refuse is easier to exercise when refusing does not destroy the ability to keep working or living. Supply dependencies, consent, and the ability to challenge rules matter in many relationships. The study uses small fictional models to clarify conditions; it does not establish outcomes for real households, organizations, or societies.

## 3. What are MonkuAi, Aperture, and Unflatten?

MonkuAi supplies part of the intellectual background, including epistemological alignment and questions about institutions and power. Aperture Mesh explores how actors with different internal rules can connect through bounded agreements, consent, and exit. Unflatten explores how to think and make decisions while retaining the origins of questions and important differences. These concerns interact; they are not three completed stages of a proven social system.

## 4. What does “unflattening” mean?

It means avoiding the premature reduction of differences to one familiar question, conclusion, or score. A decision can still be made, a hypothesis rejected, and a method changed. The point is to retain what changed, why, and what remains unresolved so that further inquiry can use it.

## 5. What is an “infinite game” here?

An orientation toward continuing play and creating further possibilities, allowing purposes, rules, relationships, and participants to change. It is not simply an extremely long contest with fixed scores, and it does not require anyone to remain in a particular relationship forever. Continued existence or novelty alone does not guarantee ethical conduct.

## 6. What do HHH and UMH mean?

HHH stands for Helpful, Honest, and Harmless. UMH is this discussion's working expression for Unaligned, Malicious, and Harmful, not a standard scientific classification. They do not label people's essential nature. The experiments' roles and resource-shortfall metrics are limited proxies; they do not measure everything those words suggest.

## 7. Why are there two papers?

They are the initial v1.0 and expanded v2.0 of one study. The first examines finite allocation decisions and local implementation boundaries. The second keeps those observations while correcting the scope and adding hypothetical futures, structured world descriptions, and conditional calculations. The shared evidence must not be counted twice as independent confirmation.

## 8. What positive results were found?

In the initial four matched comparisons where only the attacking side adopted the full text, third-party shortfall decreased in three and stayed unchanged in one. Some defender-adoption conditions improved legitimate fulfillment. Fourteen selected Aperture function checks confirmed expected properties. These are limited positive results, with important differences in environment and mechanism.

## 9. What did not work, or remained unchanged?

In one allocation environment, defensive restraint did not prevent the opponent's unapproved action, and both sides stopping could remove necessary provision. The additional 64 decisions produced identical choices across four document conditions; no incremental full-text effect was detected on that task, which already included shared approval requirements. Four Aperture checks revealed three types of missing validation at function boundaries.

## 10. Did the research prove AI safety or make attackers harmless?

No. The trials assigned fictional objectives to the same AI model; they did not test actual attackers or models trained for harmful purposes. Selective extraction of techniques, long-term learning, and other models remain open. No observed increase in the measured harm is a result within the tested scope, not a universal guarantee.

## 11. Is an opponent gaining something automatically a failure?

No. Payment for useful work or a mutually beneficial exchange is different from a gain produced by depriving others. The study separates those sources of benefit. It also separates an attacker's own adoption from indirect gains caused by restraint only on the defending side.

## 12. Can keeping things unchanged count as success?

Yes, if it means preserving activities that would otherwise be lost. But identical averages across adoption conditions, maintenance over time, and avoidance of a loss are different claims. One group's hidden loss or everyone stopping is not successful preservation merely because a summary number looks stable. Eliminating UMH is not a prerequisite for recognizing protection.

## 13. What does the F1 example actually show?

Under explicit assumptions, two workshops invest their own reserves so their production reaches their minimum needs. If equipment remains theirs to use and costs and needs stay stable, they can maintain their supply base after another workshop stops supplying, without forced central redistribution. This is a conditional model construction. It does not establish protection from physical seizure, self-sufficiency caused by Unflatten, or superiority over centralized institutions.

## 14. What happened in the 12 simulated worlds?

The model described revised supply arrangements, some new education and translation activities, and reconnection after refusal. Shortfall was zero during all three observed intervals in every condition. Some actors still lacked sufficient future production or reserves. The outcomes were model-generated descriptions, not actual activities or independent social observations. Shared norms, ample reserves, and variation under identical inputs limit causal attribution.

## 15. Does “without centralizing authority” mean no rules or coordination?

No. Actors can share rules, coordinate locally, and use common recordkeeping. The concern is permanent concentration of ultimate authority over shared rules, resources, dispute judgments, and continued participation. A key question is who can override whose decisions. The study also identifies dependence on its world narrator rather than treating the model's absence of an all-powerful center as a complete demonstration.

## 16. Who produced the work, and how can readers contribute?

The study is published by kentaroid-bot with verification and writing support from Astra in OpenAI Codex. Astra also participated in protocol development; this is not an independent evaluation and has not been peer reviewed. Readers can examine the evidence, identify assumptions that fail, propose counterexamples, or suggest a small test that makes consent, resources, and usable alternatives more explicit. Sharing a link to the reports also helps others evaluate the work.
