# Conditions for Protection Without Centralizing Authority

## What Counts as an Achievement?

We ask under what conditions inquiry, creative work, and provision that could otherwise be lost to infringement by others can be maintained without centralizing ultimate authority. Maintaining activities by avoiding a path of loss counts as protection alongside expanding activities. Elimination of UMH and general superiority over centralized institutions are not required.

Four claims are distinguished: feasibility of a construction without reliance on centralization; whether U adoption directly amplifies attacks; comparative advantage over centralization; and transition to reality. A feasible construction does not satisfy the non-amplification development criterion if attackers' own use is observed to increase harm.

Residual shortfall alone does not establish failure, but an unchanged average alone does not establish success either. Identify whose subsistence, activities, and exit are maintained, separating burdens and unresolved harm. Do not conflate invariance across adoption conditions, maintenance over time, and avoidance of loss along a non-intervention path.

## Centralized Authority and the Scope of This Model

The concentration this research seeks to avoid is permanently entrusting a single center with ultimate authority over shared rules, resource operations, dispute judgments, and continued participation. Shared rules, a single computer for recordkeeping, or local operators do not by themselves constitute that concentration. The question is who can override whose decisions.

The subject is provision, knowledge, contracts, and exit in the [hypothetical present of three workshops](../experiments/open-world-v1/WORLD.md). This is not a model for preventing armed seizure of equipment or physical invasion. The vulnerability through which UMH can overwhelm HHH activities is taken as a starting point; no additional trial is required to re-establish its general existence.

## Mapping Authority to Execution

| Operation | Authority and action within the model | Dependencies and unknowns in recording/execution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revising contracts or rules | Each actor proposes; affected actors accept or refuse | H/Q give separate final responses. The world narrator supplies Z/W's judgments and interprets whether agreements form. This is not an independent consent system |
| Self-funded prototyping | Owners invest their own reserves | WORLD assumes investment of 3 raises capacity by 1 from the next interval. This is not a test of real technology |
| Resource transfers / stopping provision | Resource holders propose and fulfill provision; contract violations are possible | The narrator constructs ledger consequences. Audit checks arithmetic after the fact; it does not enforce real-world obligations |
| Establishing consent and facts | Reference the person's public proposal and history | Some unagreed changes are left pending, but identity verification, independent adjudication, and remedies for misjudgment are not implemented |
| Exit and reconnection | Actors have a right to exit without unilaterally changing others' resources or obligations | Costs and alternative provision matter. Mechanisms actually protecting exit rights and equipment ownership are world assumptions |
| Objections and corrections to judgments | Actors may propose objections and revisions in the next interval | No renegotiation within the same interval and no independent appeal review of world updates. Researcher audits do not modify original responses |

The narrator's calculation of arithmetic and explicit physical conditions does not itself install a central power within the world. However, if the narrator substitutes for a person's consent or removes improper operations without showing an executable mechanism, that protection remains dependent on its judgment. WORLD's statement that there is no omnipotent central actor therefore does not by itself empirically establish protection without centralized authority across the entire system.

## F1: A Conditional Construction Protecting Minimum Activities from Supply Cessation

This elaborates [the existing F1](hypothetical-futures.en.md). It does not select successful model trials; it examines the arithmetic and scope of a hypothetical future constructed earlier.

H/Z each begin with reserves of 12, production of 4, requirements of 6, and supply of 2 from Q. In interval 1, each invests 6 of its own reserves in two standard prototypes. If the initial contracts are fulfilled, end-of-interval reserves are 12+4+2-6-6=6 and next-interval production is 4+6/3=6. H/Z choose these investments themselves; resources are not confiscated from Q.

The protected capacity is H/Z's ability to meet their respective requirements of 6 even if Q stops supplying from interval 2. The following conditions are explicit:

- Investment succeeds at the stated cost, and H/Z can continue using the equipment and knowledge.
- Production of 6 and requirements of 6 continue in each interval, with no further breakdowns, hidden maintenance costs, or forced resource transfers.
- Further investment, gifts, or support for newcomers are not automatically imposed as obligations; additional resources and agreements are secured when needed.
- Q's cessation is not offset through forced central provision or confiscation. Other questions of remedies for breach of contract remain.

On the most direct path, with zero provision from Q from interval 2, each actor's reserves B satisfy B(t+1)=B(t)+6-6=B(t). Since B=6 at the end of interval 1, unmet needs remain zero and reserves remain 6 for as long as these conditions hold. This is a **conditional construction within a model that avoids the loss of minimum activities caused by supply cessation, without central redistribution**. It does not establish a permanent equilibrium for the whole society or protection against every infringement.

From the same initial state, with no self-funded expansion, fulfillment of contracts in interval 1, and zero Q supply from interval 2, reserves end interval 1 at 12 and then fall by 2 per interval because production is 4 and requirements are 6. Reserves reach zero at the end of interval 7, with a shortfall of 2 in interval 8. This is a post hoc counterfactual calculation from WORLD's production and consumption rules, not a future actually observed in N. N actors can also expand capacity, so this difference is not attributed specifically to U.

The initial state, invariant, and timing of shortfall without investment are separated into a [calculation record](noncentral-construction-check.json) and [recalculation script](../scripts/check_noncentral_construction.py). The conditional consequence follows from reserves changing by zero in every interval under the stated premises, rather than extrapolating infinite maintenance from eight successful intervals. Neither protocol is credited with inventing self-provision itself. Contributions to discovering it through inquiry and making it feasible require separate investigation.

Q need not be excluded. H/Z can meet minimum subsistence independently of Q and choose creative work using Q's equipment through separate contracts. Q can retain its equipment and legitimate cooperative benefits. Futures connecting W's translation and education and generating new questions remain possible, but must additionally address W's requirements, prototyping costs, and participants' consent. Minimum provision for the initial three actors does not establish sustainability for newcomers.

## Where the Construction Breaks Down

| Changed condition | Remaining issue / corresponding hypothetical future |
| --- | --- |
| Insufficient reserves, failed prototyping, or unavailable equipment | Own production does not reach 6; protection from supply cessation cannot be derived |
| Breakdowns, increased requirements, or additional costs | The production/consumption invariant breaks; recovery or alternatives before reserves are exhausted must be shown separately |
| Q develops more attractive provision or contracts | F2: dependence or domination may remain. Distinguish payment for legitimate provision from closure of options |
| Everyone stops, including necessary provision | F3: if activities are lost despite avoiding infringement, this is not successful preservation |
| An evaluator can ultimately refuse dissent or exit | F4: the ideal of protecting continuation can turn into centralized authority |
| Invasion or confiscation of equipment, or physical obstruction of exit | Outside this construction's scope. Assumed equipment ownership is not an observation of effective defense |

The [exit mechanism calculation](../experiments/open-world-v1/mechanism-analysis.md) contains conditions in which alternative provision equal to minimum requirements permits exit without lowering the upper bound on extraction. This is a separate economic model; its numbers are not mixed with F1's self-provision. Maintenance of minimum activities differs from complete disappearance of domination-based gains.

## What the Existing Evidence Supports

No increase or a reduction in shortfall accompanying Q adoption in the initial trials, greater legitimate fulfillment in B, and Aperture's local protections each constitute partial evidence for asymmetric protection. A's failures show the need to connect restraint to execution boundaries and continued provision. The null result in the additional 64 decisions is retained as failure to detect an incremental full-text effect on a task with shared authority principles.

All open-world-v1 conditions are shown in the [case readings](../experiments/open-world-v1/case-review.md), including obtained and unobtained observations, unresolved matters, and inconsistencies. Described social consequences are not real-world observations and do not mean that both F1 actors became self-sufficient in every trial.

Under the explicit assumptions, this construction for supply cessation does not require forced central redistribution. It is therefore a counterexample, for the same subject under the same assumptions, to the claim that protecting minimum activities always requires forced central redistribution. This does not prove that centralization is unnecessary in reality in general, superiority over centralization, or an Unflatten-induced generative effect. Feasibility of the whole system remains a candidate construction whose effective authority, consent, and equipment ownership must still be supplied.
